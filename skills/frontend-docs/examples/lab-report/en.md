## Abstract

We measured the period $T$ of a simple pendulum for five string lengths $L$ and determined the gravitational acceleration $g = 9.79 \pm 0.06\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, within 0.2% of the accepted value.

## Theory

For small angles the period is

$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g = \frac{4\pi^2 L}{T^2}
$$

so a plot of $T^2$ against $L$ is a straight line of slope $4\pi^2/g$.

## Method

A steel bob hung from a 1.5 m string; for each length we timed 20 oscillations three times and averaged.

## Results

| L (m) | 20T (s) | T (s) | T² (s²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.40 | 25.4 | 1.27 | 1.61 |
| 0.60 | 31.1 | 1.55 | 2.42 |
| 0.80 | 35.8 | 1.79 | 3.20 |
| 1.00 | 40.1 | 2.01 | 4.02 |

::: note
Release angle was kept below 10° so that $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ holds to better than 0.5%.
:::

## Discussion

The linear fit gives a slope of $4.03\ \mathrm{s^2/m}$, so $g = 4\pi^2/4.03 = 9.79\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. The main uncertainty is reaction time at the stopwatch.
